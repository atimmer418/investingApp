Tier 1 — acceptance criteria already in the backlog.
Medium
Why I Picked This
FRED-194 is the strongest Tier-1 story that can actually ship and be verified in this sandbox today. The other two A/C-ready stories are gated: FRED-191 (load-perf) needs a running Capacitor build on a real device this sandbox can't provide, and FRED-195 (pig avatars) needs SVGs from the PersonalTypeshit/FRED Logo sibling directory that isn't present in this repo — I checked, it's not on disk. FRED-194 has no such gate: I confirmed all 9 target pages exist and still use the old ion-toolbar/header-inner pattern, that change-bank-account already has the reference .blue-hero-header we're standardizing on, and that a theme/ dir exists for the shared partial. Tier 1, full acceptance criteria in the backlog, no triage gap. It directly continues the settings-hub consistency thread that FRED-124 (change-bank-account) and FRED-192 (tab3) started — the natural next step on the Decision Framework (dependency chain + production-readiness impact). Medium difficulty: mechanical and well-specified with a working reference implementation, though the blast radius is wide (9 pages).
Strategic Alignment
The vision is explicit about a calm, premium, consistent surface with "polished UI everywhere, no half-migrated seams." Tab 3 (FRED-192) and its first linked page, change-bank-account (FRED-124), now wear the blue-hero-header — but the moment a user taps into any of the other 9 settings pages, they hit the old toolbar header and the hub reads as half-finished. Unifying every settings page on the one header pattern removes the last visible "old FRED" seam in the most-visited settings area, exactly the kind of consistency that builds trust heading into private beta.
Production Readiness Impact
This lifts the UI-coverage / pages-migrated side of readiness directly — 9 settings pages move fully onto the design system in one pass, and the shared-partial approach also retires duplicated gradient/cutout SCSS, cutting future drift. Low architectural risk: header markup + SCSS only, no backend, schema, auth, or behavior change, with each page's goBack() and routing preserved. The one caveat is the familiar sandbox limit — visual verification reaches a static/SCSS floor here, so the live 390×844 / 430×932 device render of all 9 headers is handed to Andrew via "Looks Good."
Implementation Options
Three ways to scope FRED-194. All three end with every settings page rendering the change-bank-account blue-hero-header; they differ in how the header SCSS is shared and how much body-styling cleanup ships in this pass. Tap one to select it; use the edit pencil on the selected card to tweak the approach before approving.
Option A Recommended
Shared SCSS partial + header swap, light body pass
Extract change-bank-account's header styles (gradient linear-gradient(90deg,#2a5ae0,#1d4ed8), the concave white &::after cutout, safe-area-inset-top, .hero-nav/.back-btn/.back-btn-spacer) into one shared partial — theme/_blue-hero-header.scss — and refactor change-bank-account to consume it (zero visual change). Swap all 9 pages' <ion-toolbar><div class="header-inner"> for the .blue-hero-header structure, each keeping its own title and goBack(). Do a light body-uniformity pass (content bg #f8fafc, consistent section-card / spacing / CTA) — and if a page's body divergence balloons, split that page's deeper restyle into a follow-up rather than block the header work (AC-4 explicitly allows this).
Pros: single source of truth (satisfies AC-3); retires duplicated header SCSS; faithful to the reference; keeps the diff focused and shippable today. Cons: deep per-page body cleanup may be deferred to a follow-up on a few pages. Risk: Low–Medium — SCSS + header markup across 9 pages; mechanical, no behavior change, but a wide surface to spot-check. Estimate: ~3–5 hrs
Option B
Full uniformity in one pass
Everything in Option A, plus a complete body restyle on all 9 pages now — section cards, spacing, and CTAs fully normalized to the FRED Style Guide on every page, no deferrals. The most thorough single-pass result: every settings page pixel-consistent in one story.
Pros: maximum consistency immediately; no follow-up story needed; AC-4 fully closed in one go. Cons: much larger diff and higher regression risk across 9 pages; slower; harder to verify without a browser in-sandbox. Risk: Medium — broad body edits widen the blast radius and the chance of a per-page visual regression. Estimate: ~6–9 hrs
Option C
Per-file header duplication (no shared partial)
Copy the header SCSS into each of the 9 pages individually instead of building a shared partial. Faster to start and keeps each page self-contained, but creates ~10 copies of the same gradient/cutout to maintain — the A/C's explicitly-discouraged fallback.
Pros: no shared-file plumbing; each page fully self-contained. Cons: violates AC-3's single-source preference; 10 copies drift apart over time; the next header tweak means editing 10 files. Risk: Medium — codifies duplication and future inconsistency; exactly the drift this story exists to remove. Estimate: ~3–4 hrs
